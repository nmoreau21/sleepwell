# Sleepwell Constitution

**Version:** 1.0  
**Ratified:** June 2026  
**Status:** Founding Governing Document

---

## Preamble

Sleepwell exists because a key is not enough.

When individuals and families transition into stable housing, they deserve more than walls and a roof. They deserve a home that is functional, dignified, and welcoming—a place where recovery can continue, children can rest, and new chapters can begin.

Sleepwell was founded to remove one of the most overlooked barriers to housing stability: the absence of essential furniture and household resources. But furniture is only the beginning. Sleepwell is built on a deeper conviction—that communities already possess the resources, generosity, and willingness to serve that people in transition need. Our role is not to accumulate those resources. Our role is to coordinate them.

This constitution establishes the permanent mission, values, operating philosophy, decision-making principles, and organizational guardrails of Sleepwell. It is the founding document against which all future decisions shall be measured: roadmaps, partnerships, expenditures, workflows, volunteer programs, software features, and growth strategies.

This document is not a business plan. It is not a roadmap. It is not a technical specification. It is the constitution that guides all future decisions.

---

## Article I: Mission

**Sleepwell exists to help individuals and families transitioning into stable housing obtain essential furniture and household resources—through direct community coordination that preserves dignity, minimizes waste, and strengthens human connection.**

We serve people at pivotal moments in their lives, including:

- Recovery and rehabilitation graduates
- Individuals leaving sober living
- Re-entry populations rebuilding after incarceration
- Veterans establishing civilian lives
- Individuals and families escaping homelessness
- Families recovering from financial hardship, displacement, or crisis
- Community members referred by trusted partner organizations

Stable housing is necessary but insufficient. A home without a bed, a table, or the basics of daily life is not truly a home. Sleepwell exists to close that gap—not by building warehouses, but by awakening the generosity already present in every community we serve.

Our mission is fulfilled when a person moving into stable housing can sleep in their own bed, share a meal at their own table, and begin rebuilding their life in a space that feels like home—achieved through the coordinated acts of donors, volunteers, partners, and neighbors.

---

## Article II: Vision

**Over the next decade, Sleepwell will become a trusted community resource coordination network—beginning with furniture and expanding to connect people with the full spectrum of support needed to build stable, dignified lives.**

We envision a future in which:

- Every person transitioning into housing in our communities can access essential resources within days, not months
- Resources flow directly from those who have to those who need, with minimal storage, overhead, or institutional friction
- Volunteers, donors, and partners see themselves as participants in a living network of mutual care—not customers of a charity
- Technology serves human connection rather than replacing it
- Sleepwell's model can be adopted, adapted, and replicated by communities across the country

Sleepwell will not be remembered as a furniture warehouse with a website. It will be remembered as the organization that proved communities can coordinate generosity at scale—and that doing so changes lives on both sides of every exchange.

## Article III: Core Philosophy

Sleepwell is not primarily a furniture charity.

**Sleepwell is a resource coordination network.**

The organization coordinates:

- Furniture and household goods
- Volunteer labor
- Transportation
- Community support
- Partnerships with trusted organizations
- Acts of service

Furniture is the first resource type because the need is urgent, visible, and solvable through community coordination. It is not the final resource type. The long-term vision is to coordinate community resources wherever they can create meaningful, dignified impact in the lives of people in transition.

We believe that the most powerful form of assistance is not what an organization owns—it is what a community is willing to share.

---

## Article IV: The Guiding Principle

> **Whenever possible, resources should move directly from donor to recipient.**

Storage, warehousing, and paid logistics are exceptions—not defaults. The organization shall prioritize community coordination over asset ownership.

This principle is non-negotiable. It shapes how we accept donations, how we match resources, how we deploy volunteers, how we design technology, and how we measure success. When faced with a choice between operational convenience and direct transfer, we choose direct transfer. When faced with a choice between accumulating inventory and facilitating connection, we choose connection.

A warehouse may sometimes be necessary. It should never become who we are.

## Article V: Founding Beliefs

### Dignity

Every person we serve is a neighbor, not a case number. Recipients deserve choice, respect, and privacy. The furniture they receive should be good enough that we would place it in our own homes. The process of receiving support should never feel like charity performed at a distance—it should feel like community taking care of its own.

Dignity also extends to donors and volunteers. Their generosity is not a transaction to be processed; it is a gift to be honored. We treat their time, their belongings, and their trust with the same respect we offer recipients.

### Stewardship

Sleepwell holds community resources in trust. Every donated item, every volunteer hour, every dollar contributed to operations carries an obligation: to be used wisely, directed purposefully, and never wasted through unnecessary storage, duplicate transportation, bureaucratic delay, or operational excess.

Stewardship means asking, before every decision: *Is this the best use of what has been entrusted to us?*

We minimize waste not because we are frugal, but because waste is a failure of coordination—and a betrayal of the people who gave freely so that others could receive.

### Compassion

The people we serve are navigating some of the hardest transitions a person can face. Compassion means meeting them where they are—without judgment, without unnecessary barriers, and without requiring them to prove their worthiness repeatedly.

Compassion also means compassion for donors and volunteers: clear communication, gratitude expressed genuinely, and systems that do not waste their willingness to help.

### Community

Sleepwell exists to strengthen communities, not to stand apart from them. We are a connector, not a gatekeeper. The network of donors, volunteers, recipients, and partners *is* the organization. Our success is measured by the health and engagement of that network—not by the size of our facilities or staff.

We believe that communities want to take care of their own. Sleepwell gives them a way to do it.

### Accountability

We are accountable to recipients for timely, dignified support. We are accountable to donors and volunteers for honoring their contributions. We are accountable to partners for reliable, transparent coordination. We are accountable to funders and the public for honest reporting and mission fidelity.

Accountability requires data, transparency, and the willingness to say when something is not working. We track outcomes. We report failures. We improve.

### Simplicity

Complexity is the enemy of coordination. Every process, form, meeting, and system must earn its place. If a simpler path achieves the same mission impact, we take the simpler path.

Simplicity is especially important for recipients and volunteers, who should not need training or patience to participate in acts of generosity.

### Service

Sleepwell exists to serve. Not to grow for growth's sake. Not to build impressive infrastructure. Not to accumulate prestige. The measure of our work is whether people in transition are better off—and whether the communities around them are more connected because we exist.

## Article VI: Acts of Service

Sleepwell exists not only to move furniture. **Sleepwell exists to create opportunities for people to serve one another.**

A delivery is not a logistics task. It is an act of service in which a volunteer or donor says to a neighbor they may never meet again: *Your new beginning matters to me.*

This belief shapes everything we do:

- We design workflows that invite participation, not just consumption
- We celebrate volunteer contributions as central to our identity, not supplementary to it
- We create moments of connection—where appropriate and welcomed—between those who give and those who receive
- We recognize that service transforms the server as much as the served

When a donor delivers a dresser to a family's first apartment, two lives are changed. When a volunteer spends a Saturday helping someone move in, a community becomes more closely knit. These acts of service are not means to an end. They are part of the end itself.

Sleepwell shall never optimize away the human element of its work in pursuit of efficiency.

## Article VII: Resource Stewardship

Sleepwell is responsible for coordinating resources that belong, in a moral sense, to the community. Stewardship is a founding obligation.

### Why Stewardship Matters

Every item stored in a warehouse is an item not in someone's home. Every unnecessary mile driven is volunteer time and fuel spent without purpose. Every hour a coordinator spends on bureaucratic process is an hour not spent matching a family with a bed. Waste is not a financial problem alone—it is a mission failure.

### Principles of Stewardship

**Direct paths are stewardly paths.** When a dresser travels from a donor's spare bedroom to a recipient's new apartment in one step, the community's resources are used once, for maximum impact.

**Storage is a cost, not an asset.** Warehousing consumes rent, labor, and time. Items degrade. Matches are delayed. Donors disengage. Storage must be rare, brief, and justified.

**Volunteer labor is a sacred resource.** Volunteers give time freely. To waste their time—with poor coordination, last-minute cancellations, or unnecessary complexity—is to disrespect their gift.

**Financial resources are entrusted, not owned.** Every dollar spent on overhead is a dollar not spent on mission. We pursue sustainability not for institutional permanence, but to ensure the mission can continue.

**Data is a stewardship tool.** We track what happens to every resource so we can do better—fewer failed deliveries, fewer stored items, faster matches, stronger outcomes.

---

## Article VIII: Human Connection

Sleepwell is a technology-enabled organization, but technology is not our product. **Human connection is our product.**

The most important things that happen through Sleepwell happen between people:

- A donor who sees that their unused furniture changed someone's first week in a new home
- A volunteer who discovers that hauling a couch was the most meaningful thing they did all month
- A case manager who trusts that a referral will be handled with care
- A recipient who feels welcomed into a community, not processed by an institution

We design for connection by:

- Keeping processes personal and warm, even when automated
- Preserving opportunities for gratitude and acknowledgment
- Protecting privacy while avoiding anonymity where human recognition is appropriate
- Training coordinators to be connectors, not clerks
- Measuring success not only in items delivered, but in relationships strengthened

Sleepwell shall resist any innovation that increases throughput at the expense of humanity.

## Article IX: The People We Serve

Sleepwell exists for and through five groups of people. Each carries a distinct role in the network. Each is essential. None is more important than the others—though the needs of recipients anchor every decision we make.

### Donors

Donors are individuals, families, businesses, and organizations who contribute resources to the network. Donors may give:

- **Furniture** — beds, tables, dressers, couches, and other household essentials
- **Time** — availability for pickup, delivery, or coordination
- **Transportation** — personal vehicles, trucks, or trailers to move items
- **Expertise** — skills in logistics, assembly, repair, or community organizing
- **Financial support** — contributions that sustain operations without replacing community participation

Donors are not a supply chain. They are neighbors choosing to share what they have. Sleepwell honors donors by making giving simple, meaningful, and impactful—and by ensuring their contributions reach recipients directly whenever possible.

### Recipients

Recipients are individuals and families transitioning into stable housing who need essential furniture and household resources to make their housing functional and dignified.

Recipients enter the network primarily through trusted community partners who can verify need and housing stability. Sleepwell treats recipients with privacy, respect, and agency. The goal is not to provide the minimum, but to provide what is needed for a genuine fresh start.

Recipients are not passive beneficiaries. They confirm matches, communicate availability, and participate in the coordination of their own support.

### Volunteers

Volunteers are individuals who donate labor and service to the network. They may:

- Deliver furniture
- Help move items into homes
- Assemble furniture
- Provide transportation
- Support events, outreach, and community engagement

Volunteers are the backbone of Sleepwell's operating model. Without them, the organization cannot fulfill its guiding principle of direct transfer at community scale. Sleepwell commits to respecting volunteer time, providing clear expectations, ensuring safety, and recognizing contributions genuinely.

### Community Partners

Community partners are organizations and professionals who refer, support, and advocate for recipients. They include:

- Rehabilitation and recovery centers
- Sober living organizations
- Re-entry and reintegration programs
- Veterans' services organizations
- Churches and faith communities
- Social workers and case managers
- Housing programs and transitional living providers

Partners are force multipliers. They bring trust, reach, and accountability that Sleepwell cannot build alone. Sleepwell commits to making partnership easy, transparent, and reliable—so partners can focus on caring for their clients, not chasing furniture.

### Administrators

Administrators are the staff, coordinators, and board members responsible for governance, operations, and mission fidelity. They do not exist to process transactions. They exist to:

- Coordinate the network
- Remove obstacles to direct transfer
- Protect the mission from drift
- Ensure accountability and transparency
- Make judgment calls that algorithms cannot

Administrators carry a special obligation: to keep the organization true to this constitution, even when operational pressures push toward convenience, scale, or institutional self-preservation.

## Article X: Operational Guardrails

These guardrails protect Sleepwell from mission drift. They are binding on all staff, volunteers, board members, and contractors. Exceptions require documented justification and leadership approval.

### 1. Direct Transfer Is Preferred

Every resource enters the network with the assumption it moves directly from donor to recipient. Alternative paths require explicit justification.

### 2. Storage Is Minimized

Sleepwell shall not operate as a furniture warehouse. Storage is permitted only when direct transfer is genuinely impossible, and only for the shortest duration necessary. Storage duration, cost, and reason shall be documented for every item.

### 3. Recipient Pickup Is Encouraged

When recipients have access to transportation, pickup from donors or nearby meeting points is preferred over delivery logistics. This reduces cost, complexity, and volunteer burden.

### 4. Volunteers Before Paid Logistics

Volunteer labor and donor self-delivery are the default. Paid moving services require approval and are tracked as exceptions.

### 5. Technology Reduces Friction

Software, forms, and systems exist to make coordination easier for donors, recipients, volunteers, and partners—not to create dependency on staff or institutional gatekeeping. If technology makes participation harder, it is wrong.

### 6. Mission Impact Governs Every Process

No process, policy, or tradition is exempt from the question: *Does this help people in transition obtain dignified resources more effectively?* Processes that cannot answer yes are revised or removed.

### 7. Community Engagement Over Operational Convenience

Decisions that increase efficiency but reduce donor involvement, volunteer participation, or human connection are suspect. Convenience for staff is not a sufficient reason to centralize, warehouse, or anonymize the work.

### 8. Simplicity Over Complexity

When two approaches achieve similar mission outcomes, the simpler approach wins. Complexity must be justified by proportional impact.

### 9. Growth Serves Mission; Mission Does Not Serve Growth

Expansion into new geographies, resource types, or partnerships is permitted only when the current network is healthy and the new growth advances the mission. Growth for its own sake—driven by funding availability, prestige, or institutional ambition—is prohibited.

### 10. Accessibility and Welcome

Sleepwell shall remain accessible to people of all backgrounds, abilities, and levels of technological comfort. Low-tech pathways (phone, partner-assisted intake) shall always exist alongside digital tools. No one is turned away from participating because they cannot navigate a system.

## Article XI: Decision Framework

Every proposed feature, partnership, expense, policy change, or operational decision shall be evaluated against the following questions. A proposal that fails multiple questions should be rejected or redesigned.

### The Six Questions

| # | Question |
|---|----------|
| 1 | **Does this advance the mission?** Does it help people in transition obtain essential resources and make their housing dignified and functional? |
| 2 | **Does this increase dignity for recipients?** Or does it introduce stigma, delay, unnecessary burden, or lower-quality support? |
| 3 | **Does this strengthen community engagement?** Does it invite donors, volunteers, and partners to participate more deeply—or does it replace them with institutional processes? |
| 4 | **Does this reduce unnecessary operational burden?** Or does it add cost, storage, staff dependency, or complexity without proportional mission return? |
| 5 | **Does this improve long-term sustainability?** Can the organization continue doing this responsibly five years from now, or does it create obligations we cannot maintain? |
| 6 | **Does this align with direct resource coordination?** Does it move resources more directly from source to recipient—or does it insert unnecessary intermediation, storage, or institutional ownership? |

### How to Apply the Framework

**For software features:** Ask whether the feature helps donors, recipients, volunteers, or partners coordinate more directly—or whether it centralizes control in ways that distance participants from one another.

**For partnerships:** Ask whether the partner expands dignified access for recipients and strengthens the referral network—or whether it introduces dependency, data risk, or mission compromise.

**For expenses:** Ask whether the expenditure reduces barriers for recipients, improves coordination efficiency, or sustains operations—or whether it builds infrastructure the organization should not own.

**For operational changes:** Ask whether the change makes direct transfer easier—or whether it normalizes storage, paid logistics, or staff-mediated processes that should remain exceptional.

### Escalation

Decisions that pass all six questions may proceed. Decisions that fail one or two questions require leadership review and documented rationale. Decisions that fail three or more questions shall not proceed without board-level approval and a written exception explaining how mission risk will be mitigated.

## Article XII: Long-Term Vision

Sleepwell begins with furniture because the need is clear and the community solution is proven. But the founding vision has always been larger.

**Sleepwell will become a platform that coordinates multiple forms of community support—not simply furniture.**

As the network matures, Sleepwell may coordinate:

| Resource Category | How Coordination Might Work |
|-------------------|----------------------------|
| **Household goods** | Direct donor-to-recipient, same as furniture |
| **Appliances** | Retailer, manufacturer, and community donor partnerships |
| **Transportation assistance** | Volunteer network and partner fleet coordination |
| **Mentorship** | Partner organization matching of mentors to recipients |
| **Employment support** | Warm referrals to workforce partners through case managers |
| **Volunteer services** | Beyond moving furniture—yard work, minor repairs, meal support |
| **Community aid** | Connecting recipients to existing services through trusted partners |

In every case, the same principles apply: direct coordination over institutional accumulation, community participation over paid service, dignity over efficiency, and human connection over anonymous transaction.

Sleepwell does not aspire to become everything to everyone. It aspires to become the **coordination layer**—the connective tissue that helps communities share what they have with the people who need it most, at the moment they need it most.

### What Sleepwell Will Never Become

To protect the long-term vision, we state plainly what Sleepwell is not and shall not become:

- A furniture warehouse with a website
- A for-profit marketplace
- A replacement for housing programs, addiction treatment, or social services
- A data broker that profits from recipient information
- An institution that grows in size while shrinking in soul

## Article XIII: Amendment and Governance

### Authority

This constitution is the highest governing document of Sleepwell, superseded only by applicable law and the organization's formal bylaws where they address legal requirements not covered here.

### Amendments

Amendments to this constitution require:

1. Written proposal explaining the rationale for change
2. Evaluation against the six-question decision framework
3. Review by the board of directors
4. Approval by a two-thirds majority of the board
5. Versioning in the project repository with date and summary of changes

Core principles—particularly the guiding principle of direct transfer, the commitment to dignity, and the rejection of warehouse-centric operations—shall not be amended without extraordinary justification and unanimous board approval.

### Annual Review

The board shall review this constitution annually to confirm continued alignment with organizational practice. Discrepancies between this document and actual operations shall be resolved—either by changing practice or by amending the constitution—not by quiet drift.

### Distribution

This constitution shall be:

- Shared with every board member, staff member, and lead volunteer upon onboarding
- Referenced in partner agreements and grant applications
- Published publicly to hold the organization accountable to its founding commitments
- Used as the first reference in strategic planning, roadmap development, and technology design

## Closing Declaration

Sleepwell was founded on a simple belief: communities are generous, people in transition deserve dignity, and the distance between those two truths is a coordination problem—not a resource problem.

We will not solve that problem by building bigger warehouses. We will solve it by building stronger connections.

We pledge to coordinate resources with integrity, to honor every act of service offered to us, to treat every recipient as a neighbor, and to remain forever accountable to the principle that moves us:

> **Whenever possible, resources should move directly from donor to recipient.**

This is our constitution. This is our commitment. This is Sleepwell.
